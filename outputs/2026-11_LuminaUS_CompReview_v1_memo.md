## 3. Above-Band Exceptions

### Marketing — L3 Marketing Manager

**Count:** 2 employees
**Avg compa-ratio:** 1.20
**Range:** 1.17–1.22

Both Marketing L3 Marketing Manager employees (EMP-4007 and EMP-4008) are paid above the 1.15 compa-ratio threshold. EMP-4007 is approximately 22% above the band midpoint; EMP-4008 is approximately 17% above the band midpoint. Both employees were hired in 2024 and are flagged as "Above band" in the source roster.

**Market context:**

The Marketing L3 band midpoint sits approximately 4.5% above the external market P50 for this role category. The two above-band employees are positioned 4.5%–8.4% above the market P75 — above market but below the 15% over-P75 flag threshold. Their pay is elevated relative to market but not dramatically so; it appears to reflect market counter-offer dynamics or competitive hiring conditions in 2024 rather than systematic band misapplication.

The two in-band Marketing L3 employees (Sr Marketing Manager title, same level) have compa-ratios of 1.07 and 1.10 — both approaching the upper boundary but within band. There is a meaningful pay spread within the Marketing L3 cohort (CR range 1.07–1.22), driven entirely by the title distinction between "Sr Marketing Manager" and "Marketing Manager." The band does not differentiate between these sub-titles; HR should confirm whether this reflects an intentional within-level hierarchy or an inconsistency in titling.

**Risk assessment:** Medium. Above-band pay is not creating an immediate cost crisis, but the absence of documented rationale is a controls gap. If these placements reflected retention counter-offers or competitive hiring, that context should be on file.

**Proposed action:** HR to (a) confirm whether documented rationale exists for both above-band placements; (b) if not, initiate comp review conversations with hiring managers to document rationale; (c) assess whether the Marketing L3 band should be refreshed given current market positioning.

## 4. Below-Band Exceptions

### G&A — L2 Finance / HR Analyst

**Count:** 1 employee (EMP-2011)
**Compa-ratio:** 0.82
**Deviation below midpoint:** approximately 18%

EMP-2011 (Finance Analyst, G&A L2) is the only formally below-band employee in the LuminaUS roster. The compa-ratio of 0.82 places this employee 18% below the band midpoint — meaningfully below the 0.85 threshold.

**Market context:**

The G&A L2 band midpoint is approximately 9% above the external market P50 for this role category, meaning Lumina's stated midpoint target is set above market median. EMP-2011's current pay positions this employee:

- Above market P25 (positively, by ~3.5%)
- Approximately 10% below market P50

While the formal market retention-risk flag (base < P25 × 0.90) is not triggered, this employee is compensated below the market median despite Lumina's band being set above market. The practical implication: a competitor offering market-median pay would represent a meaningful upgrade for this individual.

**Attrition log context:** The attrition log records a G&A L2 Finance Analyst (EMP-2016) who departed voluntarily in September 2026 for a lateral new opportunity. EMP-2016 was subsequently replaced by EMP-2011. It is unknown whether EMP-2016's departure was compensation-related, but the succession points to a pattern of turnover at G&A L2 that HR should monitor. The incoming replacement (EMP-2011) has been placed significantly below midpoint, which reproduces the conditions that may have contributed to earlier churn.

**Near-band watch:** One additional G&A L2 employee (EMP-2012, HR Generalist) has a compa-ratio of 0.89 — in band, but near the lower boundary. HR may wish to review this employee alongside EMP-2011 to avoid a second below-band outlier emerging in the next review cycle.

**Risk assessment:** Medium. Below-band at a junior level is lower flight risk than senior, but G&A L2 analyst roles are high-volume hiring positions with active market alternatives. Given the prior G&A L2 departure and the Engineering L2 comp-linked loss (see Section 5), HR should treat junior analyst retention as a near-term priority.

**Proposed action:** HR to (a) review EMP-2011's comp in context of start date, performance, and growth trajectory; (b) assess whether a band catch-up adjustment is appropriate; (c) confirm that EMP-2012's current comp trajectory will keep them above the lower band boundary through the next annual cycle; (d) consider whether the G&A L2 band midpoint needs to be updated to better reflect current market.

## 5. Attrition Cross-Reference

### Engineering L2 — Compensation-Linked Departure (Oct-26)

The October 2026 attrition log records one Engineering L2 Software Engineer (EMP-1031) who departed voluntarily. The stated reason was "Compensation — competing offer." Notes indicate the employee accepted a position offering approximately 20% above their base salary, which implies a competing package that would exceed the current Engineering L2 band maximum and sit approximately 10% above the market P75 for Software Engineer L2.

**Significance:** This signals that some portion of the top-performing Engineering L2 cohort may be receiving market offers that Lumina's current band structure cannot match without an out-of-band exception. The current Engineering L2 roster shows compa-ratios tightly clustered between 0.98 and 1.03 — well-centered, with no individual above band. However, if the competitive market has moved above the band maximum for high performers, retention pressure will remain even for in-band employees.

No other attrition log entries across the trailing three months cite compensation as a departure reason. The three Content departures in November (EMP-5009, EMP-5010, EMP-5011) were attributed to a competitor opportunity, personal reasons, and returning to school respectively — not direct compensation complaints — though the regrettable Content L3 departure (EMP-5009) to a competitor warrants HR attention outside this comp review.

**Proposed action:** HR and FP&A to review whether the Engineering L2 band maximum is competitive for top-of-band performers in the current market. This is not a broad band refresh request — the midpoint appears well-calibrated — but the ceiling may need updating to retain senior contributors within the L2 tier.

No employees triggered either of the formal market flags:
- **Above-market flag** (base > P75 × 1.15): 0 employees
- **Below-market flag** (base < P25 × 0.90): 0 employees

All five departments are broadly market-competitive. Specific observations by department:

**Engineering:** L2–L4 compa-ratios average 1.00–1.02. External benchmarks place current pay in the P50–P75 range for most levels. The competitive offer that pulled EMP-1031 suggests the market has moved above P75 for high performers at L2; this does not require immediate broad action but warrants monitoring.

**G&A:** Most levels are market-aligned. The L2 Finance/HR Analyst exception (EMP-2011) is between P25 and P50 externally — not below-market by the flag threshold, but below the median. The G&A L2 band midpoint ($107K) is set above market P50 ($98K), which is appropriate for a company of Lumina's scale but means below-band employees also fall below market median.

**Sales:** L2–L5 levels are positioned in the P50–P75 market range for Account Executives and Sr Account Executives. No market flags. The Sales L2 SDR role (EMP-3014) is addressed separately in Open Items.

**Marketing:** The two above-band Marketing L3 employees are 4.5%–8.4% above market P75 — elevated but within a defensible range for retained talent. The broader Marketing L3 cohort (all four L3 employees) averages a compa-ratio of 1.14, indicating the entire cohort leans toward the upper end of the band. A band refresh for Marketing L3 should be considered given the concentration near the ceiling.

**Content:** All active employees are in band. The three November departures left the Content L3 cohort at two employees (EMP-5006 and EMP-5007), both at or near band midpoint. No comp concerns in the remaining Content roster; however, backfill hires for the departed roles (REQ-501, REQ-502) should be brought in at or above midpoint to avoid widening a pay gap as the team rebuilds.

## 8. Open Items

**O-1 — Sales L2 band coverage gap (SDR):** EMP-3014 holds a Sales Development Representative title at L2. The Comp_Bands sheet maps Sales L2 to the Account Executive band. The source dataset flags EMP-3014 as "In band," implying a separate SDR band or agreed exception exists outside the documented band structure. HR should confirm the formal band assignment and, if an SDR band is not currently documented, create one. Absent a defined SDR band, this employee cannot be accurately assessed in future comp reviews. Note that EMP-3014's pay is also below the market P25 for Account Executives; if the AE market benchmark is used as a proxy for SDR, a market retention flag may apply.

**O-2 — Content backfills (REQ-501, REQ-502):** Two open requisitions are backfilling November Content attrition. At target start dates in Q1 2027, HR should confirm that incoming offer levels are set at or above band midpoint for the respective levels to avoid importing a below-band situation into a team already managing a 27% attrition event in one month.

**O-3 — Marketing L3 title consistency:** The Marketing L3 cohort contains both "Sr Marketing Manager" and "Marketing Manager" titles at the same level. The above-band exceptions carry the "Marketing Manager" title; the in-band employees carry "Sr Marketing Manager." This is counter-intuitive (senior titles should typically command higher pay) and may reflect either a titling inconsistency or an intentional differentiation. HR should confirm whether separate bands or sub-levels are warranted.
