**Classification:** RESTRICTED — HR / Controller access only

## Executive Summary

LuminaUS recorded **6 departures** in the trailing three months, concentrated in Content (3 in November alone) with additional exits in Engineering, Sales, and G&A. **Half were regrettable** — employees the company would have retained given the choice. Total estimated cost-to-replace is **$1,122,500**, with $888,125 attributable to the three regrettable exits.

The most acute issue is the Content team, which lost 27% of its headcount in a single month and is now operating at 73% of its approved budget. The monthly attrition rate of 31.6% far exceeds the 2%/month flag threshold. Two of the three Content departures have backfill requisitions open; one remains unposted. Without rapid remediation, survivor workload creates a secondary flight risk within the team.

Two compensation-driven concerns also surfaced: one G&A analyst is below both the comp band midpoint and the market P50 (active retention risk), and one Sales SDR is below the band floor. Both require immediate HR action.

## Trailing Three Months — All Departures

**November 2026 (Content — 3 exits)**

All three November departures were from Content and all were voluntary. EMP-5009 (L3 Sr Content Manager) left for a competitor in a new opportunity — the only regrettable exit in this cohort. EMP-5010 (Content Associate) cited personal reasons; EMP-5011 (Content Coordinator) is returning to school. The two non-regrettable exits do not indicate a systemic problem with the team's culture or management, but their timing compounds the impact of the regrettable exit significantly.

The Content team entered November at 11 employees — on budget. It exited at 8. Average headcount for the month was 9.5. Monthly attrition rate: 31.6% (departures ÷ average headcount). Annualised, that rate is 379% — a mathematical expression of the fact that the current departure pace, if sustained, would turn over the entire team multiple times per year. It will not be sustained; these are discrete events. However, it flags that three departures in one month from an 11-person team is a structural disruption, not a minor variance.

**October 2026 (Engineering — 1 exit)**

EMP-1031 (L2 Software Engineer) departed voluntarily for a 20% base salary increase from a competitor. This is a regrettable exit and a comp signal. Current Engineering L2 SEs range from $172K–$180K against a market P50 of $168K — the population is modestly above median, which is the right position. However, the departure demonstrates active external recruiting at premium rates for this role category. Monitor closely.

**September 2026 (Sales and G&A — 2 exits)**

EMP-3015 (L2 Account Executive, Sales) was an involuntary exit following a performance improvement process. Non-regrettable. The headcount has not been backfilled — the seat remains open as REQ-201, which is now 46 days past its target start date and has an offer extended without a commission plan (flagged separately in the pipeline report).

EMP-2016 (L2 Finance Analyst, G&A) departed voluntarily for a lateral move elsewhere. Regrettable. The seat was replaced by EMP-2011 at $88K — notably below the band midpoint of $107K and the market P50 of $98K. This creates a flight risk for the backfill; see the Flight Risk section.

## Flight Risk Watch — Forward-Looking

**High: G&A L2 Finance Analyst (EMP-2011)**
Hired in June 2024 as the backfill for EMP-2016 at $88K. The comp band midpoint for G&A L2 is $107K; the market P50 is $98K. At $88K, this employee sits 18% below band midpoint and 10% below market median. The same role category in G&A (EMP-2016) departed voluntarily just two months before this hire. HR should initiate a comp review for this employee before year-end. The cost of a market adjustment is substantially lower than another replacement cycle.

**High: Sales L2 SDR (EMP-3014)**
At $65K against a comp band floor of $72K and a market P25 of $68K for the Sales L2 category. This is a below-floor compensation situation regardless of whether an SDR-specific band exists. HR should verify whether a separate SDR band is configured; if not, create one. Either way, $65K is below the lowest external market quartile for this level and creates a legal equity exposure as well as a retention risk.

**Medium: Engineering L2 population**
The departure of EMP-1031 for a 20% increase signals that Engineering L2s are being recruited aggressively. The current population ($172K–$180K) is above market P50 ($168K), which is the correct positioning — but only modestly. Any employee hired before 2024 at the lower end of the range who has not received a market adjustment is a retention watch item.

**Medium: Content L3 survivors (EMP-5006, EMP-5007)**
The departure of EMP-5009 means the two remaining L3 Content Managers are now absorbing workload equivalent to a 50% increase. Both are paid above the Content L3 market P50 ($142K), which reduces comp-driven flight risk. However, workload-driven attrition is a real and independent risk. HR to conduct stay interviews within 30 days.

**Watch: Marketing L3 above-band positions (EMP-4007, EMP-4008)**
Two Marketing Managers are compensated above P75. This is not a flight risk — it is an equity and budget risk. Recommend freezing comp increases for these roles at the next cycle and documenting the business justification for above-band positioning.
